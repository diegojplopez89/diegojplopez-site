Humanitarian operations · Casa Alitas

Leadership through rapid operational growth

Diego's public record documents a progression through Casa Alitas leadership roles as the Tucson humanitarian response expanded. NPR reporting from 2019 identified him as lead coordinator during the shelter's rapid move into the former Benedictine Monastery. CBS News later interviewed him as program manager in 2021, and KJZZ identified him as program director in 2024 while Casa Alitas was handling hundreds of asylum seekers each day.

This progression reflects experience translating policy changes and fluctuating arrivals into practical decisions about shelter capacity, staffing, transportation, medical support, food, and coordination with government and community partners.

Nonprofit governance · Current public profiles

Board service

The Center for Victims of Torture currently lists Diego as a member of its Board of Directors. The organization's profile highlights his Casa Alitas experience, migration work, graduate education, and public-health interests.

Kaleidoscope Humanitarian Aid lists Diego as Board Secretary and describes his humanitarian leadership and work strengthening shelter and referral systems for asylum seekers.

Field leadership beyond one organization

Research collaboration · 2021–2025

Connecting frontline practice to research

The University of Arizona lists Diego as a co-author of a 2021 peer-reviewed article on migration, COVID-19, Indigenous communities, language justice, trauma-informed care, and border-wide health networks. A University of Arizona faculty CV also lists him as a co-author on the 2025 American Association of Geographers presentation Asylum politics and local humanitarianisms along the U.S.-Mexico border.
