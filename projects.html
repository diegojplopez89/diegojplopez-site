Selected work

Practical responses to human challenges.

A selection of work at the intersection of humanitarian care, organizational leadership, research, public policy, and useful technology.

01 · Humanitarian response & trauma-informed care

Casa Alitas: a bridge from custody to community.

As executive director of migrant services, I helped lead one of the country’s largest humanitarian reception operations for people seeking asylum after release from federal custody. Our work was not simply to provide shelter—it was to create a safe, welcoming transition toward family reunification, onward travel, and everyday life.

That meant coordinating food, temporary shelter, information, referrals, volunteers, and partnerships across a rapidly changing environment while maintaining dignity and psychological safety at the center of the response.

  • Led a $12 million humanitarian operation serving up to 1,800 people per night.
  • Supported 62 staff members and more than 600 volunteers across multiple sites.
  • Helped secure more than $13 million in public grants and philanthropic support.
  • Strengthened coordination among local government, federal agencies, faith communities, and nonprofit organizations.

02 · Human-centered systems & national referrals

Helping support follow people beyond the border.

Humanitarian assistance becomes harder when information stops at the edge of one organization’s service area. In consulting work with Catholic Charities USA, I helped develop data and referral systems connecting border-based asylum services with case-management networks in 10 interior cities.

The goal was straightforward: make the handoff between organizations more useful, track interactions and outcomes, and help communities respond with a clearer understanding of what people needed next.

  • Designed coordination and referral workflows spanning multiple communities.
  • Developed program metrics that supported operational decision-making.
  • Connected direct-service realities with practical, usable information systems.

03 · Refugee resettlement research & operational intelligence

Turning complex information into better decisions.

With Cherokee Federal, I contributed to research and operational analysis supporting displaced people and the communities receiving them. That work included assessing medical services for Afghan refugees nationwide, developing resettlement dashboards, monitoring shelter capacity, and helping organizations improve longer-term support.

I also managed a team of 10 case managers focused on employer partnerships and refugee employment across Arizona—connecting high-level analysis to the day-to-day realities of settlement.

  • Produced operational reporting used to track shelter capacity and emerging needs.
  • Assessed access to medical and community services across refugee resettlement settings.
  • Built dashboards to help identify potential resettlement locations and anticipate community capacity.

04 · Partnerships, funding & organizational leadership

Building the relationships that make response possible.

At Save the Children, I supported U.S. border-response partnerships and contributed to planning and allocation within a $7 million humanitarian response portfolio. The work brought together organizations responding to the needs of children and families across the borderlands.

Today, board and governance work continues through the Center for Victims of Torture and Kaleidoscope Humanitarian Aid, where I have contributed to policy conversations, executive leadership selection, organizational governance, bylaws, and board development.

  • Supported cross-border partner coordination and program monitoring.
  • Contributed to governance and policy work supporting survivors of torture.
  • Helped establish the governance framework for a Southern Arizona humanitarian nonprofit.
