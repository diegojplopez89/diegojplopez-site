Systems & Transformation

Turning complex service problems into workable systems

Selected examples of implementation, data, workflow, coordination, and automation work across humanitarian response, public-sector partnerships, and human services.

Operations transformation

Multi-site humanitarian operating system

Problem: rapidly changing arrivals, multiple shelter sites, hundreds of staff and volunteers, transportation needs, service coordination, and public reporting.

Approach: built practical workflows and low-cost data systems using the Google ecosystem to track arrivals, departures, demographics, travel needs, capacity, volunteer coordination, and service delivery.

Scale: a $12M operation serving up to 1,800 people nightly with 62 staff, 600+ volunteers, and up to 2,500 operational data points per day.

Multi-agency implementation

Referral and data interoperability across organizations

Problem: border shelters and destination organizations needed better continuity of information as families moved across the country.

Approach: supported shared referral workflows, common data fields, reporting practices, documentation, and cross-agency communication across Catholic and humanitarian partners.

Scale: coordination across multiple agencies with high-volume migration-service data, including work involving up to 10,000 new records per week.

Decision support

National capacity dashboards and operational reporting

Problem: decision-makers needed usable information about shelter capacity, community readiness, transportation, social services, and operational risk.

Approach: developed dashboards and executive-ready reporting for government officials and humanitarian stakeholders, combining field information with service-capacity analysis.

Result: clearer decision support for resource planning, referral-system improvement, and longer-term settlement planning.

AI & automation lab

Local-first AI orchestration for operational knowledge

Problem: organizations and complex personal workspaces lose time when knowledge is fragmented across files, tools, dashboards, and services.

Approach: developing a governed local-first AI environment that combines retrieval, local language models, automation, dashboards, tool access, and human approval gates.

Professional relevance: demonstrates hands-on experimentation with AI-enabled operations, governance, workflow automation, knowledge management, and low-cost implementation rather than AI as a purely conceptual interest.
