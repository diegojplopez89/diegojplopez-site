KJZZ · December 12, 2023

Coordinating language access during a rapid increase in shelter arrivals

Casa AlitasVolunteer coordinationLanguage accessTucson

KJZZ reported on a sharp increase in arrivals affecting migrant-service organizations in Southern Arizona as the Lukeville Port of Entry closure and changing border conditions placed additional pressure on local shelter capacity.

The story identified Diego Piña Lopez with Casa Alitas and described the organization housing large numbers of people after release from federal custody. A central operational need was language access: volunteers with interpretation skills were needed to help families navigate shelter services and onward travel during a period of unusually high volume.

The coverage provides a concrete example of the coordination challenges behind large humanitarian operations. Capacity depends not only on beds and buildings, but also on communication, volunteers, transportation, staffing, and the ability to respond quickly as arrival patterns change.
