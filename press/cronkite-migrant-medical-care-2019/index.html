Cronkite News · April 30, 2019

Independent reporting on medical coordination at Casa Alitas

Casa AlitasPublic healthHumanitarian operationsMedical coordinationTucson

Cronkite News reported on the rising cost of medical care for migrants in Southern Arizona and documented the practical health-support systems operating at Casa Alitas. The story identified Diego Pina Lopez—Diego Javier Piña Lopez—as the program coordinator working with shelter staff and volunteers to connect recently arrived families with needed medications and outside medical care.

The reporting describes Casa Alitas screening guests for medical needs, providing donated over-the-counter medications and flu vaccinations, arranging prescriptions, and coordinating transportation or outside care when a family's needs could not be addressed at the shelter itself.

For Diego's professional record, this coverage is useful because it documents an early stage of the Casa Alitas work in concrete operational terms: coordinating resources, identifying gaps, supporting families with immediate health needs, and working across volunteers, clinicians, pharmacies, public-health partners, and community donors.

It also provides contemporaneous evidence from 2019 that Diego's Casa Alitas responsibilities extended beyond shelter logistics into health access and service coordination—areas that later became part of his public-health, research, systems, and clinical social-work work.
