About Diego

A borderlands perspective, a builder’s mindset.

I was born in Nogales and raised in Tucson, where the border was never an abstract policy conversation. It was part of daily life, family, community, and the way people looked out for one another.

The long version

Care begins with paying attention.

I came to this work through community, curiosity, and a belief that people should not have to navigate hard moments alone. What began as volunteering grew into more than a decade of work in humanitarian response, nonprofit leadership, public administration, research, and social work.

At Casa Alitas, I moved from volunteer to organizational leadership and helped guide a response built with staff, volunteers, faith communities, public agencies, and partner organizations. Together, we created a place where people arriving from custody could find safety, care, practical support, and a path forward.

Since then, my work has ranged from refugee resettlement research and national referral networks to volunteer leadership, board governance, and technology that helps human-service teams make better decisions. I am most at home where people, systems, and complicated problems meet.

“Humanitarian response depends on trust—trust between volunteers, communities, and the people we serve.”

Beyond the job description

Curiosity is part of the work.

I am drawn to the places where ideas become real: a better community program, a more useful data system, a creative space for families, or a hands-on technology project. Outside work, that curiosity shows up in hiking, photography, gardening, making things, and finding new ways to understand how systems fit together.

Whether the problem involves people, policy, or a stubborn piece of technology, I tend to ask the same question: how can this work better for the people who depend on it?
