Biography

Diego Javier Piña Lopez, LMSW, MSW, MPA

Humanitarian and nonprofit leader, Licensed Master Social Worker, researcher, board member, and systems builder whose work spans complex operations, migration and asylum response, clinical social work, public health, organizational strategy, partnerships, governance, and technology-enabled systems.

Leadership profile

Diego's professional background combines senior human-services leadership with direct-practice social work and systems implementation. His work has included humanitarian shelter operations, cross-agency coordination, program design, public communication, referral and data systems, community partnerships, research collaboration, and nonprofit governance.

The combination is particularly relevant to organizations that need leaders who can move between strategy and execution: understanding human needs, coordinating people and partners, improving systems, interpreting evidence, and implementing practical solutions in complex environments.

Humanitarian leadership and migration

Independent reporting documents Diego's progression through multiple Casa Alitas leadership roles as Tucson's humanitarian reception system expanded and adapted to major changes in arrival volume and federal policy. His work has involved shelter operations, cross-agency coordination, humanitarian reception, public communication, and systems that help newly arrived families connect with sponsors and communities across the United States.

Licensed Master Social Worker and clinical practice

Diego is an LMSW (Licensed Master Social Worker). Psychology Today currently lists Diego Piña Lopez as a clinical social worker/therapist with the LMSW credential in Tucson, and Amanah Bloom lists Diego Piña Lopez, LMSW, among its counseling and consulting team. The Psychology Today listing describes work with migrants, refugees, underserved communities, fathers and perinatal concerns, and clients age 8 and older.

This direct-practice experience adds a person-centered clinical perspective to Diego's broader work in organizational leadership, public health, migration systems, and community services.

Research and public health

Diego co-authored the peer-reviewed article Health Sciences Interprofessional Collaborative: A Perspective on Migration, COVID-19, and the Impact on Indigenous Communities, published in Frontiers in Sociology in 2021. A University of Arizona faculty record also lists him as a co-author on a 2025 American Association of Geographers presentation concerning asylum politics and local humanitarianisms along the U.S.-Mexico border.

Board and professional service

The Center for Victims of Torture lists Diego as a current board member, and Kaleidoscope Humanitarian Aid lists him as Board Secretary. Historical professional biographies also document earlier service on the NASW Arizona Board and co-chairing the Refugee Council USA Asylum Task Force.
