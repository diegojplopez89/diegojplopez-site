Biography

Diego Javier Piña Lopez, MSW, MPA

Humanitarian leader, social worker, researcher, and nonprofit strategist whose work has focused on migration, asylum response, public health, organizational leadership, and practical systems for communities navigating crisis.

Humanitarian leadership and migration

Diego's professional work has centered on helping organizations respond to migration with dignity, coordination, and practical support. His leadership experience includes Casa Alitas in Tucson, Arizona, where he advanced through multiple roles as the shelter system expanded to receive large numbers of asylum-seeking families released from federal custody.

Independent reporting and organizational profiles document his work on shelter operations, cross-agency coordination, humanitarian reception, and systems that help newly arrived families connect safely with sponsors and communities across the United States. The Press & Media archive organizes this public record and links directly to the original publishers.

Research and public health

Diego's academic interests connect frontline humanitarian practice with public health. He co-authored the peer-reviewed article Health Sciences Interprofessional Collaborative: A Perspective on Migration, COVID-19, and the Impact on Indigenous Communities, published in Frontiers in Sociology in 2021. The article examines migration at the U.S.–Mexico border, Indigenous asylum seekers, language justice, trauma-informed care, and community-based humanitarian response.

Community and board service

Public organizational profiles also document Diego's service beyond direct shelter operations. The Center for Victims of Torture lists him as a board member, and Kaleidoscope Humanitarian Aid lists him as Board Secretary. These roles reflect a continuing interest in survivor-centered services, migration, and community-based humanitarian response.
